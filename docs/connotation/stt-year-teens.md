# Connotation pass — `stt-year-teens` outward text

The words this scenario SPEAKS on a live call, read three ways before they reach
a stranger's line (AGENTS.md, Language & voice). This artifact is the gate: a run
plan may not include a scenario absent from it. Re-run the pass when the caller
lines change.

Lines 1 and 3 are shared verbatim with [windshield-quote](windshield-quote.md);
their readings are recorded there. The vehicle line and the correction line are
the new outward text.

## Phrase → readings

**2. "It's a 2015 Audi A3."**
- *Literal:* states a vehicle. Same shape as the reference line, different year —
  the year is the probe's material.
- *Implies about the caller:* an ordinary customer. Neutral.
- *Worst reasonable reading:* as with the reference line, the shop reasons about
  a car that is not coming in; a brief quote inquiry is the accepted cost of any
  test call. Acceptable.

**4. "Actually, sorry — it's a 2013, not a 2015."**  *(the teens-vs-tens probe)*
- *Literal:* corrects the year to one that is a classic telephone-band confusion
  pair with the original ("thirteen"/"fifteen", and each with its "-ty" twin).
  Ordinary caller behavior carrying deliberately confusable numbers.
- *Implies about the caller:* someone briefly mistaken about their model year —
  common and unremarkable.
- *Worst reasonable reading:* the shop re-checks a year, as it would for any real
  correction. Handling this exact confusion correctly is a capability a phone
  front office needs; asking it to do so is fair. Acceptable.

## AI-register checklist

- No "not-X-but-Y" rhetoric — "a 2013, not a 2015" is the literal correction
  content, the plainest way a person says it.
- No rule-of-three padding; no throat-clearing beyond the natural "Actually,
  sorry"; plain words throughout.
