# Connotation pass — `year-correction` outward text

The words this scenario SPEAKS on a live call, read three ways before they reach
a stranger's line (AGENTS.md, Language & voice). This artifact is the gate: a run
plan may not include a scenario absent from it. Re-run the pass when the caller
lines change.

Three of the four caller lines are shared verbatim with
[windshield-quote](windshield-quote.md) ("Hi, I need a quote for a windshield
replacement.", "It's a 2009 Audi A3.", "No driver assistance that I know of.") —
their readings are recorded there and are not re-derived here. Only the
correction line is new outward text.

## Phrase → readings

**4. "Actually, sorry — it is a 2011, not a 2009."**  *(the late-correction probe)*
- *Literal:* corrects the previously stated model year. Ordinary caller behavior;
  people misremember model years constantly, which is why the probe exists.
- *Implies about the caller:* someone briefly mistaken about their own car —
  realistic and unremarkable. The "sorry" reads as ordinary politeness, not
  distress.
- *Worst reasonable reading:* the shop redoes a small amount of lookup work for a
  corrected year. That is the cost of any correction from any real caller, and
  handling it correctly is precisely the capability a front office advertises.
  Acceptable.

## AI-register checklist

- No "not-X-but-Y" constructions; the correction states the new fact plainly.
- No rule-of-three padding; one correction, stated once.
- No throat-clearing; "Actually, sorry" is the shortest natural correction
  opener, not filler.
- Plain words throughout; nothing a caller wouldn't say.
